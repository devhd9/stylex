HOW TO WRITE WINNING PROPOSALS
-----------

STRUCTURE (use this template, customize every time):

  Line 1: Hook — show you understand THEIR problem
    "Hi [name], I see you need to automate your [specific process]
    so your team can focus on [their actual goal, not the task]."

  Line 2-3: Relevant proof
    "I recently built a similar system that [specific result].
    Here's a quick demo: [Loom link or portfolio link]"

  Line 4-5: Your approach (brief)
    "Here's how I'd approach this:
    1. [First step — shows you've thought about it]
    2. [Second step]
    3. [Deliverable]"

  Line 6: Soft CTA
    "Happy to jump on a quick 15-min call to understand your workflow
    better and give you a more specific plan. No commitment."

  RULES:
    - NEVER start with "Dear Hiring Manager" or "I am a developer with..."
    - NEVER copy-paste the same proposal — customize the first 2 lines
    - ALWAYS include a relevant portfolio link or Loom video
    - Keep it under 150 words (clients skim)
    - Use their name if visible
    - Ask a smart question about their business (shows you read the post)

  WHAT JOBS TO BID ON (in priority order):
    1. AI chatbot / AI assistant projects (your portfolio project #1)
    2. Workflow automation (Make.com, Zapier, n8n)
    3. "Help me integrate AI into my business" (vague = high margin)
    4. Web apps that need AI features added (bridge your old skills)
    5. AVOID: "Build me a ChatGPT clone for $200" (low budget, low value)

  PRICING STRATEGY for first 3-5 AI jobs:
    - Price SLIGHTLY below market to win jobs and build AI-specific reviews
    - Aim for $500-$2,000 per project for the first few
    - After 3-5 completed AI projects with 5-star reviews, raise to
      $2,000-$5,000+ per project
    - After 10 AI projects, move to $5,000-$15,000+ per project
    - ALWAYS try to propose fixed-price over hourly (better margins)

CRITICAL MISTAKES TO AVOID
================================================================================

  1. DON'T spend 2 months learning before taking clients.
     Learn for 2 weeks, then learn BY doing client work.

  2. DON'T underprice to win jobs. $10-15/hr AI work attracts terrible
     clients. Start at $25-30/hr and bid FIXED-PRICE to control margins.

  3. DON'T stay a generalist. "I do AI and also web dev and also
     mobile apps" = nobody remembers you. Pick a lane by month 2.

  4. DON'T ignore existing web dev clients. Upsell them:
     "Hey, I've added AI automation to my services. Want me to add
     an AI chatbot to the site I built for you?" Easy wins.

  5. DON'T skip the retainer pitch. One-off projects are a treadmill.
     Retainers are where financial stability comes from.

  6. DON'T abandon Upwork too early. It's your reliable deal flow
     until your personal brand generates enough inbound leads.

  7. DON'T build in silence. Share everything you learn publicly.
     The content compounds over months and brings clients to you.
